About the Day

AI is already in Connecticut classrooms. What it does for learning depends on the people who design it into the school day. On September 25, the ACES Center for AI brings teachers, coaches, and school and district leaders together for a full day on keeping people at the center of that design.

The morning opens with Intelligence, Amplified: Four Voices, One Future, a 90-minute keynote experience connecting industry, education, and student perspectives. Breakout sessions follow in three blocks, chosen through a statewide call for proposals and led by Connecticut educators, with a hands-on Innovation Lab running all day.

The Keynote Experience

One stage, four perspectives, and an animatronic host.

Ballroom · 9:00 – 10:30 a.m.

Intelligence, Amplified: Four Voices, One Future

SparkE, a character created by Garner Holt Productions, joins virtually as the thread between speakers. The morning moves from an opening conversation on AI and animatronics with the Garner Holt team, to an industry keynote from Dr. Joshua Hunsberger, to an educator keynote from Dr. Keri MacLean, to a learner segment with Dr. Frank LaBanca and one of his Chase Academy students. A live panel closes the morning on the human possibilities behind artificial intelligence.

Garner Holt Productions + SparkE

Industry opening

SparkE, an animatronic character from Garner Holt Productions, hosts the keynote virtually. The Garner Holt team opens the morning in conversation with SparkE about where AI meets animatronics, then SparkE carries the program from voice to voice.

Dr. Joshua Hunsberger

Founder and Principal, Joshua Hunsberger LLC

An innovation strategist building partnerships across biotechnology, artificial intelligence, immersive technologies, and workforce development. He serves on the leadership team of a $160 million National Science Foundation Regional Innovation Engine, has helped secure or lead more than $30 million in funded initiatives, and holds a PhD in Neuroscience from Yale with more than 45 peer-reviewed publications. His work builds regional hubs where students, educators, and industry turn emerging technology into new career paths.

Dr. Keri MacLean

Assistant Superintendent, Cromwell Public Schools

A Connecticut public school educator with 26 years of experience, from high school English teacher to Secondary ELA Curriculum Supervisor to Director of Curriculum, including 14 years in central office administration. She holds degrees from Siena College and Wesleyan University and a doctorate from Southern Connecticut State University. Her work centers on strategic planning, curriculum design, and administrative coaching.

Dr. Frank LaBanca

Principal, ACES Chase Academy Interdistrict Magnet School

An award-winning educator, researcher, and school leader, and the 2026 Magnet Schools of America National Principal of the Year. He was Founding Principal of Danbury Westside Middle School Academy Magnet and Director of the Center for 21st Century Skills, leading STEM programs in 50 middle and high schools. His honors include the RadioShack National Teacher Award, Google Certified Innovator designation, and the CAS Outstanding Principal Award. He takes the stage with one of his Chase Academy students.

Breakout Sessions

Sessions run in 3 blocks of 6 concurrent rooms across the four strands. Proposals came in through a statewide call, and the slate below is led by Connecticut educators and leaders doing this work right now.

Designing Learning

Beyond the Tool: From AI Tools to AI Thinking in the Classroom

Lauren Sweeney, Norwalk Public Schools

Watch how fifth graders test and revise their own writing prompts with AI, then walk away with strategies for weaving AI thinking into any elementary subject.

Designing Learning

AI as a Thinking Partner in Secondary Education

Lisa Lassen, Amity Regional District No. 5

Amity's team shows how students in grades 7-12 use AI feedback as a mirror for their own thinking, with the district guidelines that keep the practice honest.

Designing Human Agency

CTRL+Human: Putting Students in Charge of AI-Enhanced Learning

Joanne Mumley, Wolcott Public Schools

A 2026 Presidential AI Challenge state winner shares 3 classroom routines (Be the Bot, Hot Seat, Sandbox) that put students in the decision-maker's seat with AI.

Designing Learning

AI Under the Hood: Bringing the 5 Big Ideas to Life for Middle Schoolers

Cheryl Tokarski, STEM Alliance of Connecticut

Get hands-on with a free, open-source curriculum that teaches middle schoolers how AI actually works, and join a statewide network of educators building it together.

Designing Systems

AI Powered, Human-Centered: A Systems Approach to District Implementation

Thomas Scarice, Westport Public Schools

Westport's superintendent walks through how his district built AI into strategic planning, instruction, and operations over a full year, and hands you the roadmap.

Designing Systems

Building AI Champions

TJ Neville, Farmington Public Schools

How one district grows internal capacity by developing teacher leaders who carry AI practice back into their buildings.

Designing Human Agency

Agency First, AI Second: Strategic Frameworks for Human-Centered District Innovation

Michelle Eckler, Stratford Public Schools

Frameworks that keep educator and student agency at the front of district innovation decisions instead of letting the tools set the agenda.

Designing Human Agency

Teacher as Creative Director

Alan Grazioso, independent consultant and executive producer

A producer's take on the teacher's new role: directing the creative work while AI handles production, and what that shift asks of instruction.

Designing Human Agency

Engineering Intent: How to Scaffold Cognitive Thinking in the AI Era

Module Downer, Stamford Public Schools

Practical scaffolds that keep the cognitive lift with students when AI is available for every step of the task.

Designing Systems

AI and Curriculum Writing: How District-Created Gems Transformed Curriculum Writing and Feedback

Andrea Drewry, Amity Region 5

Inside the custom district-built assistants that changed how Amity writes curriculum and gives feedback to writing teams.

Designing Intelligent Tools

Exploring Ozobots in the Classroom

Megan DaCosta, Windsor Public Schools

A hands-on look at bringing robotics and computational thinking into everyday classroom routines.
